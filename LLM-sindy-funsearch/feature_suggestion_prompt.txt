BACKGROUND AND CONTEXT
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

This project studies dengue fever dynamics in the Dominican Republic at the provincial level. The goal is not just forecasting. The goal is discovering the true governing equations of dengue transmission, equations that are sparse, interpretable, and biologically meaningful, and then using those equations for forward simulation and public health resource allocation.

WHAT WE HAVE BUILT SO FAR AND KEY FINDINGS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Step 1. SINDy baseline (Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics).
We engineered a 66-term polynomial library from lagged incidence values, rolling means, the Extrinsic Incubation Period (EIP = exp(0.347 * temp - 9.17)), cyclical seasonal encoding (week_sin, week_cos), temperature lags, rainfall lags, humidity, and their pairwise interaction terms. We ran SINDy per province and globally. Key biological finding: temperature never appeared as a standalone term. It only appeared as a multiplicative interaction with incidence features. This suggests temperature modulates transmission momentum rather than independently driving new cases.

Step 2. FunSearch with geographic island clustering.
We split provinces into coastal and inland islands and ran LLM-guided equation search. The coastal island equation did not use rainfall at all. The inland island equation used rainfall lag and humidity terms. The model independently discovered that coastal provinces are temperature-driven while inland provinces are rainfall-sensitive. This aligns with known Aedes aegypti habitat preferences.

Step 3. GA-SINDy with Bayesian-optimized hyperparameters.
We ran a genetic algorithm with simulated annealing over parallel islands. An LLM mutator proposed structural changes to equations each epoch. Best discovered equation: dI/dt = -0.22 incidence_lag4 + 0.09 temp_lag4 times rolling_incidence_4wk - 0.06 incidence_lag1 times temp_lag4 + 0.03 incidence_lag1 times EIP - 0.02 temp_lag4 times incidence_lag2. Mean test R-squared of 0.54 across 11 quality-passing provinces. The model plateaued early because the fixed 66-term library was exhausted.

WHAT WE ARE BUILDING NEXT
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

We are building a SINDy Autoencoder pipeline. Here is the full architecture.

1. Reshape input.csv into 288 province-year chunks. Each chunk is 52 weeks times all features for one province in one year. Each chunk is one independent outbreak episode.

2. Train an autoencoder on these 288 chunks. The encoder compresses each 52-week episode into 3 latent coordinates. The decoder reconstructs the original episode from those 3 numbers. The autoencoder is trained by minimizing reconstruction error.

3. Visualize the 288 latent points in 3D space. Each province-year becomes a point. Cluster structure will reveal outbreak regimes and province groupings.

4. Run SINDy in latent space. Find sparse governing equations for how the latent coordinates evolve over time. The library in latent space is small and automatic, all polynomials of degree 2 over 3 variables.

5. Evolve equations using a genetic algorithm with parallel islands and an LLM mutator. The LLM operates with rich biological context and mutation history. Dynamic library expansion allows the LLM to propose entirely new functional forms beyond the polynomial basis.

6. Decode the best latent equation back through the decoder to recover dI/dt in the original feature space.

7. Use the validated equations to project forward 4 to 8 weeks and generate a future tensor of province by week by features. Use this for resource allocation optimization and climate scenario simulation.
