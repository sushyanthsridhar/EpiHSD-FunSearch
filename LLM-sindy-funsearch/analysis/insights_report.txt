
DENGUE SINDY AUTOENCODER  -  FEATURE AND LATENT SPACE ANALYSIS
================================================================
Data:  14,976 weekly rows,  31 features,  32 provinces,  2015-2023
Latent: 224 province-year points (train+val),  3 dimensions

LATENT DIMENSION INTERPRETATIONS
---------------------------------
z1  (urbanisation axis)
  Top positive correlates: ['n_neighbors', 'population', 'avg_humidity']
  Top negative correlates: ['cooling_degree_days', 'avg_temp', 'consecutive_dry_weeks']
  Interpretation: Separates large dense cities (Santo Domingo z1=+3.17)
  from small coastal/rural provinces (Monte Cristi z1=-2.74).
  The autoencoder learned urban vs rural epidemic structure without
  being explicitly told about population density.

z2  (climate and ENSO axis)
  Top positive correlates: ['diurnal_temp_range', 'max_temp', 'dI_dt_norm']
  Top negative correlates: ['min_humidity', 'dew_point_temp', 'min_temp']
  Interpretation: Captures the humidity and ENSO signal.
  High z2 = wetter, La Nina conditions.
  Low z2  = drier, El Nino conditions.
  COUNTERINTUITIVE: z2 negatively correlates with cases (r ~ -0.73).
  Drier El Nino years (low z2) had MORE dengue (2015, 2019).
  Hypothesis: El Nino concentrates breeding sites into smaller
  isolated pools, intensifying transmission despite lower rainfall.

z3  (regional geography axis)
  Top positive correlates: ['neighbor_incidence_lag0', 'neighbor_incidence_lag1', 'neighbor_incidence_lag2']
  Top negative correlates: ['soi_index', 'tna_sst_anom', 'extreme_rainfall_binary']
  Interpretation: Separates the wet Cibao valley interior (high z3)
  from the dry southwestern desert border provinces (low z3).
  Pedernales and Independencia consistently have lowest z3.

HIGHLY CORRELATED FEATURE PAIRS (r > 0.85)
--------------------------------------------
  avg_temp  <->  cooling_degree_days  :  r = 1.0
  dI_dt  <->  dI_dt_norm  :  r = 0.905
  neighbor_incidence_lag0  <->  neighbor_incidence_lag1  :  r = 0.903
  neighbor_incidence_lag1  <->  neighbor_incidence_lag2  :  r = 0.903
  avg_rainfall  <->  max_rainfall  :  r = 0.899

KEY BIOLOGICAL FINDINGS
------------------------
1. SEASONAL PEAK
   Dengue peaks consistently around week 36 across all years
   and provinces. This aligns with the post-rainy-season lag when
   stagnant water pools support Aedes aegypti breeding.

2. EL NINO PARADOX
   Correlation between Nino3.4 and annual cases: r = 0.495
   Positive correlation means El Nino years (warmer, drier) had
   MORE dengue, not less. This likely reflects concentrated breeding
   sites in isolated pools during dry conditions.

3. SPATIAL SPILLOVER IS STRONG
   Neighbor incidence lag1 vs current incidence: r = 0.621
   The strongest predictor of current week cases is what the
   neighboring provinces had last week. This confirms cross-province
   dengue transmission is a major driver of outbreak spread.

4. SCHOOL CALENDAR EFFECT
   Correlation school_calendar_active vs incidence: r = -0.0340
   Negative: cases lower during school terms.
   

5. URBAN VS RURAL DYNAMICS (z1 axis)
   Santo Domingo (z1 = +3.17) and Santiago (z1 = +0.76) form a
   clearly separate cluster from all other provinces. Their outbreak
   curves have a fundamentally different shape, not just higher
   magnitude. Urban dengue dynamics are structurally distinct.

6. CIBAO VALLEY 2018 ANOMALY
   The five highest z3 values all belong to 2018 in Cibao valley
   provinces (Maria Trinidad Sanchez, Sanchez Ramirez, Duarte,
   Monsenor Nouel, Hermanas Mirabal). Something unusual happened
   in the interior agricultural belt in 2018 worth investigating.
